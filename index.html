What Clarv does

Fourteen things a seller's spreadsheet cannot.

01

Reconciliation that ties

A sub-ledger per marketplace account that balances to the paisa against the bank and posts as one balanced journal, not a mismatch report.

02

Fee audit

Every referral, closing, weight-handling and storage fee checked against a dated rate card, with the claim drafted.

03

Claim windows on a calendar

Lost, damaged and unreturned inventory with the days left to claim, so reimbursements are not left on the table.

04

Warehouse stock, unit by unit

Your stock reconciled against the marketplace's own inventory ledger: what was received, sold, returned, adjusted and never reimbursed.

05

Stock transfers done right for GST

Challan, e-way bill JSON, the marketplace's transfer report and the sale from the right GSTIN as one chain.

06

Marketplace invoices in your GSTR-2B

Every fee and advertising invoice matched document by document, so input credit is claimed once and never missed.

07

TCS and TDS proven

GST TCS and section 194-O TDS set against your returns and Form 26AS, entity by entity.

08

Exports as one chain

Cross-border sale → shipping bill → IGST refund → FIRC → GSTR-1 table 6A.

09

Receivable ageing and payout forecast

What each marketplace owes you, by account and by reason, and when it lands.

10

True contribution margin

Landed cost from your purchase receipts, not typed-in guesses; margin after fees, after ads, per SKU.

11

One thread per order

Order to voucher on one page, with a "why" on every figure.

12

Account health and ODR, measured

Order defect, cancellation, late dispatch and tracking rates from your own records, beside the marketplace's.

13

Stock planning and price floor

Days of cover, reorder points, aged inventory from the marketplace's report; a floor price from cost, fees and GST.

14

Yours, and priced as software

Your data isolated per seller on AWS; nothing sold, shared or blended. Your accountant works in the same place you do.

How it stays honest

Every figure names its source. Every check says what it could not verify.

  • Reads the marketplaces' own reports and APIs; never guesses a column, and names the ones it did not know.
  • A month closes only when its settlements tie and the bank shows the deposits. Nothing posts into a closed month.
  • Rules and rate cards are authored and dated by chartered accountants; nothing learns from a click.
  • Writes to a marketplace only on a person's approval. Reads are the default.
